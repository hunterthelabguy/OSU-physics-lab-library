# Lab 1 – Team Building

## PH211 – General Physics with Calculus

**Activity goal:** **Build community and learn how this course will work.**

Working with a team is one of the most crucial skills in physics and other technical disciplines. You will likely collaborate with many people throughout your academic and non-academic careers. Employers frequently cite the ability to work well on a team as one of the most desirable traits they look for in candidates. In this lab course, all of the work is done in groups as well.

The learning objectives below give a summary of the key skills for teamwork. If you run into issues with group dynamics at any point in the course, don’t hesitate to ask your instructor for help.

## Learning Objectives

**Learning goal: Build and practice skills for successful group work.**

This lab activity is designed to help you practice the following skills:

1.  Reflect on team dynamics
2.  Identify behaviors that create a collaborative work environment
3.  Develop consensus within a group
4.  Foster group problem solving
5.  Practice professional conduct
6.  Engage respectfully with others’ ideas and contributions
7.  Distribute work effectively among group members

## Part 0: Introduction to Lab

Your instructor will review how lab will work - making groups, turning in reports, grading, etc.

## Part 1: Inclusive and Collaborative Lab Environment

Your instructor will hand out one notecard to each of you. Title one side “Included” and the other side “Excluded”.

::: action
- Recall or imagine a time when you came into a room – maybe a physics class – where other people were gathered in a group taking part in an activity.
- **Individually**, consider what actions from the group or aspects of the environment would make you feel included and able to join and contribute to the activity. Write your ideas on the “Included” side.
- Consider what actions from the group or aspects of the environment would make you feel excluded and unable to join and contribute to the activity and write them on the “Excluded” side.
- When you’re done, your instructor will collect your cards and lead your class in a discussion about inclusivity.
- At the end of the discussion, respond to the prompt below with your lab group.
:::

::: {.question #ph211-team-building:good-lab-partner}
**Describe the qualities and actions of a good lab partner in your lab report. Discuss how your group came to a consensus on your final response.**
:::

Your instructor will collect the responses and share them with the class.

## Part 2: Team Challenge

Your instructor will describe a structure that you and your group will attempt to build this week. They will distribute the materials you will have available and the describe the goal of the challenge.

::: action
- Individually, draw a sketch of how you might build the structure. You’ll include this drawing in the lab report.
- Once everyone has some preliminary ideas, discuss your individual drawings and ideas with your group members. Together, make a plan for how you will build your structure and make one sketch of your team’s design.
:::

::: {.question #ph211-team-building:sketches}
**Add everyone’s individual sketches (identified by team member) as well as the team’s final plan.**
:::

::: action
- Begin building. If you find your plan isn’t working, feel free to change your design as you go. When you have a structure you like, call your instructor over for a height measurement.
:::

::: hint
- You can continue to work on the structure and ask for additional height checks. Only your highest will count.
:::

When you finish your construction (or run out of time) respond to the following questions in your lab report.

::: {.question #ph211-team-building:build-process}
**Describe the process you used to build your structure. Include the things that you tried that *didn’t* work. Why did you try them? What did you change about your planning after those ideas did not work out?**
:::

::: {.question #ph211-team-building:contributions}
**Identify the members of your group who suggested each of the main features of your structure, and how each member contributed to its construction.**
:::

**[Clean up anything you dropped on the floor (e.g., broken spaghetti).]{.underline}**

When you’re done with your responses, discuss how you will present your structure to the class. Decide who in your group will do this. What will you do if your structure no longer exists?

## Part 3: Presentations

Your group will briefly present your structure to the class, focusing on aspects of your structure you felt made it successful. Possible discussion points include:

::: action
- What design choices did you make, and why?
- How did your design choices turn out? What worked? What didn’t?
- What unanticipated challenges did you run into, and how did you adapt to them?
:::

## Part 4: Reflection

*After* having listened to other groups present their structures, reflect on these questions as a group.

::: {.question #ph211-team-building:rebuild-changes}
**What things might you try if you were to build the structure again. What sort of changes would you make? Why?**
:::

::: hint
- For those ideas from other groups that you would want to try, [say whose idea it was]{.underline}. Talk to that group and get the names of its members. When doing science, you should **not** borrow ideas from others without giving them credit. Good science builds on the ideas of others *and* acknowledges their contributions.
:::
