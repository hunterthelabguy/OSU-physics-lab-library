# General Physics Problem-Solving Strategy

Dr. Rebecka Tumblin – General Physics with Calculus

Below we outline a general physics problem-solving strategy that can be applied to any physics problem you encounter. It is recommended that students practice this problem-solving strategy and apply it to each and every physics problem they approach. Over time, you will notice that you spend less time struggling to start problems and arrive at a more accurate solution with less effort.

## General Problem-Solving Strategy

1.  **Understand, Visualize and Plan**
    - Read the problem carefully.
    - Sketch the situation and draw an appropriate diagram (the sketch and diagram are different!).
    - Establish a coordinate system you will use for solving the problem.
    - Make a table of known and unknown information and the symbols you will use to represent the information.
    - Label your sketch with the symbols in your table.
    - Identify important simplifications or assumptions you will make to solve the problem.
2.  **Solve the Problem Symbolically**
    - Represent the general physical laws symbolically.
    - Specify the law for the particular problem using knowns from STEP 1.
    - Do not plug-in numbers as you go (unless it’s zero).
    - Solve the problem symbolically using the appropriate physical laws.
    - Show all work.
    - Write your final answer **only** in terms of the symbols representing the known quantities you listed in STEP 1.
    - Plug in numerical values (if given) at the end.
    - Include the physical units with any numerical answer.
3.  **Make Sense of Your Solution**
    - Does your solution accurately reflect nature?
    - Perform a **dimensional analysis** and explicitly check the physical units of your symbolic solution.
    - Use one or more **sensemaking techniques** discussed in the course to stress-test the validity of your solution.

## Example: Applying the General Problem-Solving Strategy to a Physics Problem

**Problem Statement:** A car of mass $m$ is traveling up a hill of angle $\theta$ at a speed $v_o$, when suddenly the car slams on its breaks and skids to a stop to avoid hitting a squirrel. The coefficient of kinetic friction between the tires and the road is $\mu_k$. The acceleration due to gravity near the surface of the earth is $g$. Determine a symbolic expression for the distance the car slides up the hill from the moment the car begins to slide.
